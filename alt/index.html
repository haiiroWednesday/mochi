The company’s president told the Chronicle the switch followed Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr.’s push against synthetic dyes. His technicians swapped FD&C Blue No. 1 for spirulina, a pigment pressed from algae.

Plants can paint the warm colors. Mint sits in the gap.

Where food colors land on the rainbow, synthetic dyes against their plant and algae replacements

The six synthetic dyes most foods used each hit one sharp shade, and together they cover the rainbow from red to indigo.

Their plant replacements pile up at the warm end. Beet, paprika, carrot, annatto and turmeric all land somewhere in red, orange or yellow.

Nothing on the plant side lands on green. Food green is usually mixed from a blue and a yellow, which is how It’s-It builds its mint, and why the blue it picks decides the shade.

The blues each come with a catch. Spirulina fades when heated, butterfly pea turns purple in acid, and galdieria was approved only in May 2025. Mars launched its new M&M’s with no blue at all.

Note: dots are placed by approximate hue to show the spread, not measured from samples. Sources: FDA color additive listings; HHS and FDA, May 9, 2025; Food Research International, 2021; Mars, 2026; San Francisco Chronicle, 2026.

It’s-It is one of many. Makers of 90 percent of U.S. grocery-store ice cream have pledged to drop synthetic dyes by 2028, and Mars launched dye-free M&M’s short two colors.

General Mills has been here before, and children noticed first.

Blue and green have left the bowl before

One bowl of Trix, refilled for each year

2015
Six colors, all from synthetic dyes.
General Mills switched to colors from fruit, vegetables and spices. In its taste tests, kids asked one thing.“What did you do with my cereal? Where’s the blue and green?”
Sales of the new Trix beat expectations, and Classic Trix came back anyway after fans complained. One called the plant version “basically a salad.”
Synthetic dyes are out again. The color line on the box now readsColor (vegetable and fruit juice, annatto extract, turmeric extract and other color added)No blue is listed. General Mills hasn’t said why.

It’s-It’s website still shows the old green. Its president admitted the original color would probably be better, “because people know it.”
