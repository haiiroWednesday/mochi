Why Is My Mint Ice Cream Less Green?

Across America, Food Makers Are Rebuilding Familiar Colors

By Cynthia2 min read
It’s-It mint

Before2026

SAN FRANCISCOFor forty years, the mint in an It’s-It, the ice cream sandwich the city has eaten since 1928, was one seafoam green. This May the green faded so far that one teenager took his for vanilla, his parent wrote on Reddit.

J.D. Morris, a Chronicle reporter who grew up on It’s-Its, swears the flavor faded with the color, into “the La Croix version of mint.” The company says the peppermint never changed. Only the blue did.

Seven dyes ran the grocery aisle
Spread

FDA tests and certifies every batch of these dyes before it can be sold, so a cereal plant in Minnesota and an ice cream plant near San Francisco could count on the same intense, predictable Blue No. 1.

2026

This year the machine cracked. Only Red 3 is banned, starting in 2027, yet companies are dropping the rest too after the Trump administration’s health secretary, Robert F. Kennedy Jr., asked food makers to phase them out by the end of 2027. A California law keeps them out of school meals from that date too.

Split

Each old dye was one stable molecule that looked the same in a sour drink or a hot oven. Plant colors react to the food around them, so below the crack every food needs its own recipe. Fat in its icing meant one Sam’s Club cookie needed thirty times more natural color, NPR reported.

The thin outline around each food is its old color.

It’s-It has company. Makers of 90 percent of U.S. grocery-store ice cream have pledged to drop the same dyes by 2028.

Where the new blue gets stuck

Peppermint has no color. It’s-It’s green came from a blue laid over yellow-orange annatto. For decades that blue was Blue No. 1. Its algae replacement, spirulina, hits a different wall in each food.

Ice cream

In ice cream the wall is taste. Whipped-in air and fat lighten any color, and enough spirulina to win the green back can bring off-flavors. It’s-It’s technicians wanted color “without an aftertaste.”

Cereal

General Mills said in 2016 that Trix needs color so bright that plant colors had to go in heavy. At that dose they “can bring with them some off flavors” and cost more. Cereal also gets cooked, and heat fades spirulina’s blue.

Candy shell

M&M’s shells are sprayed on by machine, and spirulina left a goopy foam that was hard to clean out. Mars bought more than 300 new machines and cleaning tools, The Wall Street Journal reported, and its dye-free M&M’s still launched two colors short.

Shoppers remember each food differently. For M&M’s, it’s a set of six. For Trix, it’s whether blue and green show up at all.

New blues were approved in 2025. The 2026 bowl still has none.

For It’s-It, it’s one shade. Few customers have complained and mint sales held, its president says. Still, he admitted the original color would probably be better, “because people know it.”
