Why Is My Mint Ice Cream Less Green?

Across America, Food Makers Are Rebuilding Familiar Colors

By Cynthia2 min read

SAN FRANCISCOIn 2018, Chronicle staff matched paint chips to the mint in a papier-mâché It’s-It. This May the green faded so far that one teenager took his for vanilla, his parent wrote on Reddit.

The company’s president told the Chronicle the switch followed Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr.’s push against synthetic dyes. Technicians swapped Blue No. 1 for spirulina, an algae pigment, seeking color “without an aftertaste.”

Six dyes ran the grocery aisle

Each jar is one synthetic dye. The pipes lead to foods FDA lets it color.

Spread

FDA lets Blue No. 1 color candy, drinks, cereal, ice cream and frosting. A small set of standardized dyes gave a cereal plant in Minnesota and an ice cream plant near San Francisco the same intense, predictable color to design around.

2026

This year the machine cracked. All six dyes are still legal, yet companies are dropping them anyway after the Trump administration’s health secretary, Robert F. Kennedy Jr., asked food makers to phase them out by the end of 2027. A California law keeps them out of school meals from that date too.

Split

Below the crack the six dyes break unevenly. Some get one plant source, some get two, and none of them swaps in everywhere: at Sam’s Club, a frosted cookie needed thirty times more natural color, NPR reported, because of the fat in its icing. Plant green fades fast and FDA allows it in almost nothing, so green gets mixed from blue and yellow. The blue candy shells have nothing yet.

The thin outline around each food is its old color.

Makers of 90 percent of U.S. grocery-store ice cream have pledged to drop the same dyes by 2028. One blue, though, hits a different wall in each food.

One blue, three foods

Blue No. 1 went into ice cream, cereal and candy shells the same way. Its algae replacement, spirulina, has to get past a different wall in each food.

Ice cream

In ice cream the wall is taste. Whipped-in air and fat lighten any color, and enough spirulina to win the green back can bring off-flavors. It’s-It’s technicians wanted color “without an aftertaste,” and the filling came out pale.

It’s-It hasn’t published its recipe, so the slider shows the trade-off, not the real amounts.

Cereal

Trix needs color so bright that plant colors had to go in heavy, General Mills said in 2016, and at those levels they “can bring with them some off flavors” and cost more. That year blue and green dropped out of the box.

Candy shell

M&M’s shells are sprayed on by machine, and spirulina left a goopy foam that was hard to clean out. Mars bought more than 300 new machines and cleaning tools, The Wall Street Journal reported, and its dye-free M&M’s still launched without blue or brown.

Shoppers remember each food differently. For M&M’s, it’s a set of six.

For Trix, it’s whether blue and green show up at all.

Trix had no blue or green in 2016. New blues arrived in 2025.

For It’s-It, it’s one shade, repeated since the 1980s. Its website still shows the old green, and its president admitted the original would probably be better, “because people know it.”
