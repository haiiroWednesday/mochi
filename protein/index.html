Why Is There Protein in My Soda?

Protein Left the Supplement Aisle. Now It’s on Almost Every Shelf.

By Cynthia2 min read
Packages that say protein
0of 12
Scroll to stock the shelf

Santa ClaraThis month Cheez-It is putting a new box on shelves: the same orange cracker, now with 7 grams of protein per serving. It joins protein Cheerios, protein Pop-Tarts, protein Doritos and a soda with 30 grams of protein. On Instacart, searches for “protein” rose 12.7 percent in a year.

No aisle is safe
Before

Protein used to have three homes: the supplement shelf, the meat case and the dairy case.

Breakfast

Then it moved into breakfast and the freezer. On Instacart, Cheerios Protein’s share of sales grew 157 percent in a year. Eggo Protein waffles grew 101 percent.

Growth in each product’s share of items sold on Instacart, June 2025 to June 2026.

Newcomers

Newer products grew even faster. Pop-Tarts Protein’s share rose 542 percent from December 2025 to June 2026. Protein instant potatoes rose 87 percent.

2026

This year protein reached the chips, the crackers and the soda. Doritos Protein arrived in March. Cheez-It Protein reaches stores this month.

That is the loud half of the boom. Instacart’s sales data shows a quieter half.

What shoppers actually added

Instacart ranked the 20 high-protein foods that gained the most share of its sales in the first half of 2026. Greek yogurt, eggs and milk lead the list.

Added protein

Only two of the 20 are products built around added protein: protein drinks and protein bars.

Always had it

The other 18 had protein all along. Chicken, cottage cheese, tuna, beans. Nobody had to put a sticker on them.

Fastest

The fastest climber was canned sardines, up 51 percent.

Percentages are growth in each food’s share of items sold.

Young adults lead the trend. Pew found that 37 percent of adults under 30 take protein supplements, compared with 23 percent of those 65 and older.

Much of the added protein is whey, the liquid left over from making cheese.

Small cheesemakers used to spread whey on their fields or feed it to pigs. Now the protein-rich kind costs more than twice what it did a year ago.
