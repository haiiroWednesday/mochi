In 1980, 44 percent of American college students said they had downed five or more drinks in a row at least once in the previous two weeks. By 2024 that share had fallen to 20 percent, the lowest the University of Michigan’s Monitoring the Future survey has ever recorded for college students.

Drinking of any kind fell along with it, and in 2024 nearly half of college students said they had not had a single drink in the past 30 days, which also set a record. Before you see the real numbers, try drawing the line yourself.

66% of Americans 18 to 34 told Gallup in 2025 that even moderate drinking is bad for your health, up from 52 percent two years earlier. In January 2025 the U.S. surgeon general asked for cancer warnings on alcohol labels.

Gallup, 2023 and 2025 · U.S. Surgeon General, 2025

People ages 15 to 24 spent about 150 minutes a day with friends in person in 2003 and about 40 minutes in 2020, the surgeon general reported.

Among adults 19 to 30, past-month cannabis use rose from 18.5 percent in 2014 to 29% in 2024, while past-month drinking fell from 68 percent to 62 percent.

Monitoring the Future panel study, 2025, via WRD News

NielsenIQ, 2025

In a December 2023 survey, 61% of Gen Z adults 21 and older said they planned to drink less in 2024, up from 40 percent a year earlier, and 36 percent named mental health as their reason.

NCSolutions survey, 2024

GENERATION × DIVERSITY世代多様性

Young adults are more racially and ethnically diverse than they were 20 years ago, and Gallup notes that non-white Americans drink less at every age. That shift explains part of the decline, Gallup found, though not all of it.

Gallup, 2023

Fewer hangouts may matter most of all, because a drink usually needs company. The surgeon general’s numbers cover everyone ages 15 to 24, students included, and they show in-person time with friends dropping by almost three-quarters in under two decades.

None of this means college parties have disappeared. College students are still the heavier drinkers of their age group, and one in five binge drank in 2024. Yet the share who sat out the whole month keeps climbing, and for many of the students counted in these surveys, a sober Friday has become the ordinary one.
